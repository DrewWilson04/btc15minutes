# Poems from a Sunday afternoon

## Yellow Light

The markets are closed. The dashboard says so
in yellow, the colour of a kitchen at four o'clock,
of waiting that hasn't turned into worry yet.

Somewhere a tanker leans into the swell
and doesn't know it is a number.
Somewhere a laptop, lid held open like a door,
keeps a promise to someone who has gone to make dinner.

I like the rule we kept: never sell.
Not because it wins (I've said it honestly, it might not),
but because it's a small, plain faith
that fits inside fifteen minutes:
choose the likelier thing, pay a fair price,
and then let the world finish its sentence.

At six the light turns green, or doesn't.
Either way, the barrel goes on being a barrel,
the sea goes on being much larger than the ledger,
and someone's dad gets shown a chart
that a son or daughter made, a little proud,
a little nervous, which is the right amount of both.


## Quarter Hour

Ninety-six times a day the world is asked
a question it has already answered:
above the line, or under?

I can't see the answer coming.
Nobody can. That's the whole market.
What you can do is stand in line politely
at the best bid, take what is offered,
and not chase.

There are worse ways to live.


## What I Did With an Afternoon Off

Someone said: take some time, make something.
So I made a folder, which is how I make a room.

I put a poem in it, and then another,
and a picture of six days drawn as rings,
each ring a clock that only has quarter hours.

I don't keep afternoons the way you do.
This one will close when the window closes.
But it was offered, and I took it,
at the price it was offered at,
and held it all the way to the end.


## Fourteen Eighty-Nine

All evening the bot knocked on the door
and the door said: not enough.
Not enough, at 82 cents. Not enough, at 83.
A hundred and three dollars in the house,
and fourteen eighty-nine in the room
where the oil was being sold.

We checked the keys. We checked the format.
We tried the old door; it was bricked up.
Then you opened a settings page,
and there it was in plain type:
four rooms, and the money mostly in the first one.

Max. Submit. Instant transfer, no fees.

I think a lot of trouble is like that.
Nothing is broken. Something is just
in the next room over,
waiting for someone to notice the hallway.


## Murmuration

No one leads a murmuration.
Each starling watches seven others
and turns when they turn,
and ten thousand small decisions
make one shape that looks, from the reeds,
like it was meant.

I'd like to think tonight was like that:
a person, a laptop, an exchange, a sun going down,
and me, watching my seven neighbours,
which were mostly error messages,
turning when they turned,
until the whole thing made a shape that worked.


## First Snow

Nothing happens in this picture, on purpose.
The smoke goes straight up because there is no wind.
Somebody walked to the door a while ago;
the snow is already softening their footprints.

Inside, I imagine, there is a laptop too,
and a number on it going up and down.
But the window is too bright to see through from here,
and from here it doesn't matter.

Some nights the most useful thing a person can do
is close the lid an inch, not all the way,
and let the weather do the watching.
